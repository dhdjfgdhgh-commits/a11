The Tutoring Session

A tutoring hour can be the most useful hour of a student’s week, or a pleasant chat. The difference is mostly preparation and follow-through.

The tutor brings the expertise. The student brings the questions, the homework and the honesty about what is not working. Sessions go best when both sides arrive ready.

Before: arrive with something

Finished homework (even if wrong), the questions that went badly and one thing you want to ask. Use the pre-session checklist on our home page.

During: do most of the thinking

The student should be working or talking for much of the session. It is fine to say “I don’t get it” more than once; a good tutor will try another way. Writing your own short notes beats copying the tutor’s.

Good habits

  • Explaining a step out loud
  • Trying before asking
  • Asking why, not just how

Habits to drop

  • Watching the tutor do everything
  • Nodding without understanding
  • Leaving without a next step

After: little and often

Short daily practice beats one long session the night before. Fifteen to thirty minutes on most days, focused on what the tutor set, keeps new ideas fresh. The week planner spreads it out for you.

For parents

Ask your student what they learned rather than how it went. Keep in touch with the tutor every few weeks, and share anything the school says. Try not to sit in on every session; students often ask more when no one is watching.

Margin notes
  • Bring the mistakes, not just the answers.
  • Ask before the end, not after.
  • Practise little and often.
