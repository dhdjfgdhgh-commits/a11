How to Choose a Tutor

The right tutor is not always the most qualified one. It is the person who can find exactly where a student is stuck and explain it in a way that clicks.

Start with a clear reason. “Better at maths” is hard to tutor; “ready for the algebra test in March” or “confident with essay structure” is something a tutor can plan for and you can measure.

1. Know the subject and the level

Look for a tutor who knows the course your student is on, including the exam board or curriculum if there is one. Subject knowledge matters, but so does knowing what the exam actually asks.

2. Ask the right questions

  • How would you find out where my child is stuck?
  • What does a typical session look like?
  • How will you tell me about progress?
  • What do you expect between sessions?
  • What happens if a session is missed?

3. Watch the trial lesson

In a good first session the tutor asks more than they talk, checks understanding with questions instead of “does that make sense?”, and ends with a clear next step. The student should feel a little challenged and not lost.

4. Red flags

Be wary of a tutor who promises specific grades, does the homework for the student, cannot explain their plan, or never asks what the school is teaching. Safety matters too: for younger students, sessions in a shared space or with a parent nearby are sensible, and online sessions should run on a platform you are comfortable with.

5. Give it time, then review

One session rarely shows much. After four to six, ask your student whether things feel clearer and look for the signs on our home page. If nothing has moved, talk to the tutor before deciding to change.

Margin notes
  • Write the goal down before you search.
  • Ask for a trial lesson.
  • Judge after 4–6 sessions, not one.
